✨ What Is phone-agent?

phone-agent is a small software package that works together with a tiny piece of hardware called an ESP32-S3. Think of the ESP32-S3 as a remote control that talks to your iPhone over Bluetooth. The phone-agent software tells that remote control what to do, and an AI assistant decides the steps for you.

Because the board pretends to be a normal Bluetooth mouse and keyboard, your iPhone accepts it without any special setup on the phone side. That means your assistant can tap buttons, type text and move around the screen just like you would — while a small RGB light on pin GPIO48 shows what is happening at any moment.

The assistant app itself is built with SwiftUI, Apple's modern interface toolkit, which is why it feels smooth and native on the iPhone. You do not need to know anything about SwiftUI to use it. You only need to follow the steps on this page.

📥 Step-by-Step Setup

  1. Download the files. Visit the phone-agent download page and save the application files into the folder you created on your computer.
  2. Connect the board. Plug the ESP32-S3 board into a USB port on your computer using the USB-C cable. Wait a few seconds while Windows recognises the new device.
  3. Start the setup tool. Open the downloaded folder and double-click the setup tool inside it. If Windows asks whether you want to allow the app, choose Yes so it can talk to the board.
  4. Choose the board. The setup tool lists the USB ports it can see. Pick the one that appeared after you plugged the board in. If you are unsure, unplug the board, note which entry disappears, plug it back in, and select that entry.
  5. Install the firmware. Press the install button and let the tool finish. It writes the mouse, keyboard and GPIO48 RGB light code onto the board. This usually takes under a minute. Do not unplug the cable while it is working.
  6. Wait for the light. When the firmware is written, the RGB light on the board glows to show it is ready. A slow pulsing colour means it is waiting for a connection.
  7. Pair with your iPhone. On the iPhone, open Settings, go to Bluetooth, and look for the new device that appears in the list. Tap it and confirm the pairing request.
  8. Open the assistant. Launch the phone-agent app on your iPhone. It will detect the board automatically and show a green connected status.
  9. Try a first command. Ask the assistant something simple, such as opening an app or drafting a short message. You will see the light on the board change colour as each action runs.
Good to know: you only install the firmware once. After that, the board remembers it. On future days you just plug the board into any USB power source, pair the phone, and open the app.

🎨 Understanding the RGB Light

The small light on pin GPIO48 is your quickest way to know what the system is doing. You do not need to open any settings to read it — just glance at the board.

  • Slow pulse: the board is powered on and waiting to be paired.
  • Steady glow: the iPhone is connected and the assistant is idle.
  • Quick flicker: the assistant is sending a mouse movement or keystroke right now.
  • Brief colour change: a command finished successfully.
  • Light off: the board has no power, or the cable is connected to a charger that does not supply enough current.

You can change the brightness and the colour palette of the light from inside the phone-agent app, so it suits your desk and your eyes.

🖱 How the Assistant Controls Your Phone

Every action the assistant takes is delivered as a standard Bluetooth mouse or keyboard event. Your iPhone treats it exactly like a person tapping the screen or typing on a paired keyboard. This is why no jailbreak, no special permissions and no developer account are required on the phone.

The assistant decides what to do, the phone-agent software turns that decision into a sequence of taps and keystrokes, and the ESP32-S3 sends them over the air. The whole round trip usually finishes in a fraction of a second, so it feels instant.

🛠 Troubleshooting

The setup tool does not see my board.

Try a different USB cable first — charging-only cables are the most common cause. Then try a different USB port, preferably one directly on the computer rather than on a hub. Unplugging and replugging the board also helps.

The board is connected but my iPhone cannot find it.

Make sure Bluetooth is switched on and that the phone is within two metres of the board. Turn Bluetooth off and back on again on the iPhone, then look at the list once more. If the board's light is not pulsing, it is not in pairing mode.

The light never turns on.

This usually means the board is not receiving enough power. Use a port directly on the computer or a powered USB hub, and check that the cable is firmly pushed into both ends.

Commands stop working after a while.

Open the phone-agent app and reconnect to the board from the device list. Keeping the iPhone screen unlocked during a long session also helps the system stay responsive.

I want to start over from scratch.

Run the setup tool again and choose the install option. It replaces whatever is on the board with a fresh copy of the firmware, and your iPhone will reconnect automatically afterwards.

💡 Tips for a Smooth First Week

  • Keep the board plugged in whenever you want to use the assistant — it has no battery of its own by default.
  • Place the board on the same side of your desk as your phone so the Bluetooth signal is never blocked by your body.
  • Rename the board in the phone-agent app so it is easy to recognise if you ever own more than one.
  • Use the RGB light as a health check. If it is dark when it should be pulsing, the problem is almost always the cable.
  • Write down the folder where you saved the setup files. You will want it again if you buy a second board.
